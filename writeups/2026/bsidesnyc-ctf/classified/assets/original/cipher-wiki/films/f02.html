The Lisbon Frequency is film #2 in the CIPHER franchise (1967), directed by Gerald Forsythe and starring Hugh Cavender as Declan Ward.

Plot

A Sentinel double agent is feeding HEXON nuclear facility locations. Ward must identify the mole using a list of dead-drop coordinates embedded in classified diplomatic cables. The trail leads to Lisbon, where a dying Portuguese intelligence officer refers to the organisation as "the Hostile Engagement and Exploitation Operations Nexus — what the Americans are calling HEXON." No subsequent film references this expansion. Fan consensus marks it soft canon, contested. The film introduces HEXON's courier network and the concept of steganographic message chains.

Section 13 Equipment

Gadgets & Technology
  • Dead-drop cipher system — a book code using standard Penguin paperback editions; specific titles communicated through a pre-arranged contact protocol.
  • Reel-to-reel tape recorder — concealed in a reinforced briefcase with a false floor; voice-activated, up to four hours.
  • Frequency scanner watch — detects active transmission bands within a 200-metre radius; displayed on a secondary dial calibrated to look like a standard watch complication.
Vehicles
  • 1966 Citroën DS — field-acquired; Section 13 had pre-modified a pool of vehicles across European cities. This one features a hydraulic suspension override for high-speed stability.
Weapons
  • Dart pistol — single shot, anaesthetic payload, concealed within the body of a Montblanc-style fountain pen.
