'Established by royal warrant in 1952' — what does that mean legally?
BrumbyFilm — 15 Feb 2024, 14:00
The article says Sentinel was established by royal warrant in 1952. In UK constitutional terms, a royal warrant establishes official recognition, not legal authority. An organisation with genuine intelligence powers would need primary legislation or an Order in Council. A royal warrant alone wouldn't give Sentinel the legal basis to do what it does. Is this a worldbuilding oversight or is it intentional — Sentinel operating outside any legal framework? — BrumbyFilm
IsabellaKT — 15 Feb 2024, 14:55
Intentional, I think. The franchise has always presented Sentinel as operating in a space between legality and necessity. The royal warrant gives it the appearance of authority without the accountability of parliamentary legislation. That's the point. — IsabellaKT
Morthwaite — 15 Feb 2024, 15:30
There are real precedents for UK government bodies operating under instruments other than primary legislation. Some of them are quite significant. The legal ambiguity is not implausible. — Morthwaite
The 2047 seal date — what's the significance?
GlassEelFan — 20 Feb 2024, 09:00
Sentinel's founding records are sealed until 2047. In the UK, the standard period for sensitive government records is 30 years, though some are extended to 50. 2047 would be 95 years from 1952. That's not a standard classification period. Whatever's in those records, someone wanted them kept away from anyone currently alive. — GlassEelFan
pellucid_k — 20 Feb 2024, 09:45
The Last Directorate suggests the reason: Sentinel's complicity in HEXON's founding. If that's accurate in-universe, the extended seal makes sense. You'd want any living participants dead before the records surface. — pellucid_k
ClaireD1965 — 20 Feb 2024, 10:20
95-year classification periods do exist in real UK government practice. Not commonly. But they exist. I read about this somewhere and can't remember where. — ClaireD1965
