Reading back some of the 1977 reviews of Ivory Gambit — the hostility toward Mace is pretty striking. 'A working-class imposter in a gentleman's role' is an actual quote from a national newspaper. Feels very of its time. — BrumbyFilm
IsabellaKT — 3 Dec 2023, 14:45
Very of its time. The Cavender era had a strong patrician quality and I think there was a genuine cultural anxiety about losing that. Ward as an aristocrat vs Ward as someone who had to earn his place. Which is ironic given the character's actual backstory. — IsabellaKT

The orphan-who-chooses-loyalty reads completely differently with Mace than with Cavender. Cavender's Ward chose it from a position of cold remove. Mace's Ward chose it because he genuinely has nowhere else to go. Both valid readings of the same character. — GlassEelFan

Chess notation cipher — is it a real system?
pellucid_k — 10 Dec 2023, 09:00
The chess notation cipher in Ivory Gambit — has anyone actually worked out whether it functions as a real encryption method? The codebook-embedded-in-game-annotations approach seems theoretically sound to me. A sufficiently large database of annotated games would make an extremely high-entropy one-time pad equivalent. — pellucid_k
Morthwaite — 10 Dec 2023, 09:55
It functions. The method is a variant of book cipher using positional notation rather than page/line/word. The entropy is actually quite good given the state space of chess. The weakness is key distribution — both parties need the same annotated game, which limits the system's practicality. — Morthwaite
WardWatcher_99 — 10 Dec 2023, 10:30
I love this wiki. — WardWatcher_99
ClaireD1965 — 10 Dec 2023, 11:00
The grandmaster in the film — Petrov, I think, though he's not named in the credits — is played by an actor who was actually a rated chess player. You can tell from how he holds the pieces. The film got that right too. — ClaireD1965
