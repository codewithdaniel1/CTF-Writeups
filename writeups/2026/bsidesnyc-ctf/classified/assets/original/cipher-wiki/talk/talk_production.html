Cayman subsidiary — what does "dormant" actually mean here?
CryptoHistoryFan5 March 2024, 13:02 (UTC)

The article mentions a Cayman Islands subsidiary described as dormant in a 2019 Companies House filing. I want to flag that "dormant" in this context has a specific legal meaning that doesn't necessarily mean "inactive." A dormant company can still hold assets, intellectual property, or rights — it simply means it hasn't conducted any "significant accounting transactions" in the relevant period. A company holding a long-term financing arrangement might legitimately file as dormant while still being operationally significant to whatever it's connected to.

ProductionGeek_UK5 March 2024, 13:48 (UTC)

That's a useful clarification. So the Cayman subsidiary being dormant doesn't tell us it's inactive — just that it hasn't transacted recently in a recordable way. Which is consistent with it being a shell holding entity of some kind.

CryptoHistoryFan5 March 2024, 14:22 (UTC)

Exactly. And a dormant subsidiary that's been maintained for decades without being dissolved is interesting. If it genuinely served no purpose you'd wind it up. Maintaining it costs money and administrative effort. Someone thinks it still has a function.

T_Ashford_19525 March 2024, 14:41 (UTC)

There are structures which are maintained not because they are currently active but because they may need to be activated. A dormant company is a door that has not yet been opened. This does not mean no one holds the key.

T_Ashford you have a way of saying things that sounds entirely reasonable and leaves me feeling vaguely unsettled. I appreciate it and I'm not sure I should.
