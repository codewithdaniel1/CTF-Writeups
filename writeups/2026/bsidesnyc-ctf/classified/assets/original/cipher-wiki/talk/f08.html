Bauer's debut — reassessment
IsabellaKT — 2 Feb 2024, 14:00
I want to propose a reassessment section for the article. After the Wall was poorly received commercially but has aged extraordinarily well. Bauer's Ward is uncomfortable in a very specific way that felt wrong in 1991 but feels exactly right now. He looks like someone doing a job they believe in for an institution they've stopped trusting. Which is very contemporary. — IsabellaKT
GlassEelFan — 2 Feb 2024, 14:55
The post-Cold War disorientation is the whole film. Everyone in it — Ward, Control, the Berlin contacts — is operating on institutional momentum without a clear mission anymore. Bauer captures that perfectly. The slight foreignness works because everyone in that film is slightly foreign to their own circumstances. — GlassEelFan
BrumbyFilm — 2 Feb 2024, 15:45
The Miriam Alade scene. The handover. It's a two-minute scene that does more character work for Control than the previous three films combined. Stroud was always background. Alade arrives fully formed. The standing ovation at the premiere was deserved. — BrumbyFilm
The hard drive MacGuffin — was it plausible?
WardWatcher_99 — 9 Feb 2024, 20:00
A 40MB hard drive as the entire HEXON archive. In 1991 this was cutting-edge. Today it's a joke. But in 1991 a 40MB drive would have cost several hundred pounds and was genuinely high-capacity. The film's grasp of the technology is correct for its moment. — WardWatcher_99
pellucid_k — 10 Feb 2024, 08:00
The encryption described in the film — 'proprietary Section 13 algorithm' — is vague but the fact that it's software-based rather than hardware-based is exactly how encryption was moving in 1991. DES was still dominant but organisations were beginning to implement proprietary layering above it. The film's instinct was right even if the detail is thin. — pellucid_k
Morthwaite — 10 Feb 2024, 08:45
The Toshiba T3200 prop is real. That model was used by British government departments in this period. I've seen procurement records. — Morthwaite
WardWatcher_99 — 10 Feb 2024, 09:30
Morthwaite. Government procurement records. Of course you have. — WardWatcher_99
Berlin locations
ClaireD1965 — 15 Feb 2024, 11:00
The film was shot in Berlin in late 1990, which means some of the location work captures the city in a uniquely transitional state. You can see buildings that still have GDR signage. There are moments in the background that feel genuinely documentary. Does anyone know if the production did any location work east of what was the wall? — ClaireD1965
pellucid_k — 15 Feb 2024, 11:45
They did. The archive scenes were shot in Prenzlauer Berg, which was still very much East Berlin aesthetically at that point. The production had unusual access for a film crew in early reunification Berlin. The location releases were apparently straightforward, which surprised the production team's own location manager according to a behind-the-scenes booklet from the VHS release. — pellucid_k
