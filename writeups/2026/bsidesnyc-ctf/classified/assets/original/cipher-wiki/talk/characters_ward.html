The name — ward of the state
IsabellaKT — 5 Mar 2024, 14:00
The article covers this well but I want to note something that I think deserves emphasis: the name Ward is one of the most elegant pieces of character design in the franchise. It's not metaphorical. It's literal. He was administratively designated a ward. The franchise turned a clerical label into a complete character philosophy. — IsabellaKT
GlassEelFan — 5 Mar 2024, 14:55
And the codename CIPHER doubles it. A cipher is also someone of no importance, a nobody. Ward is literally named Nobody. Every film in the franchise is about Nobody choosing to matter. — GlassEelFan
BrumbyFilm — 5 Mar 2024, 15:40
I've read thousands of words of film criticism about CIPHER and nobody has ever written that paragraph before. Please write it up for the article. — BrumbyFilm
Which actor gave the most complete portrayal?
WardWatcher_99 — 10 Mar 2024, 19:00
The character article raises this implicitly but doesn't adjudicate. I will: Calloway. Four films, complete arc, and he understood something the others didn't — that Ward's loyalty is a choice he has to keep making. It's not settled. He has to choose it again every morning. That's what Calloway plays. — WardWatcher_99
ClaireD1965 — 10 Mar 2024, 19:55

The 'D' in the records — is it definitely Declan?
Morthwaite — 15 Mar 2024, 08:00
The article says Agent Alone 'implies' the D stands for Declan. I've watched the relevant scene four times. The implication is extremely indirect — Ward is asked his name by a checkpoint guard and very slightly hesitates before giving it. That's all. 'Declan' is never spoken in the film. The hesitation could imply many things. — Morthwaite
GlassEelFan — 15 Mar 2024, 09:00
You're right that it's indirect. The 'Declan' reading comes from the novelisation and was then backported into fan canon. The article should be clearer that it's implied-plus-novelisation, not confirmed-in-film. — GlassEelFan
Morthwaite — 15 Mar 2024, 09:45
Updating the article accordingly. The uncertainty is actually more interesting than the answer. — Morthwaite
