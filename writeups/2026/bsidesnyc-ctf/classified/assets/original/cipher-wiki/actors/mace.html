Roland James Mace (born 22 July 1944) is a Scottish actor who portrayed Declan Ward in three CIPHER films between 1977 and 1986. His interpretation — warmer, more physically expressive, and considerably more charming than Hugh Cavender's — divided fans at the time but has since been reassessed as among the franchise's most entertaining eras.

Early life and education

Mace was born in Edinburgh's Leith district, the youngest of four children of a dockworker and a part-time seamstress. He attended George Heriot's School on a bursary, where a drama teacher recognised his natural charisma and encouraged him toward acting. He briefly attended Edinburgh College of Art, intending to study sculpture, before abandoning the course after a year to pursue acting professionally.

He worked as a labourer, a barman, and briefly as a merchant sailor before his first professional stage role in 1966 — a path that gave his screen presence what several critics have described as an authentically physical quality.

Career

Mace's early career consisted of supporting roles in British television dramas and two low-budget film productions before he came to the attention of Meridian Pictures' casting director. His selection as Ward's second actor was announced in 1976, prompting a media debate about the appropriateness of replacing a beloved incumbent — a dynamic that has repeated with every subsequent recasting.

The Ivory Gambit was a significant box-office success and introduced a lighter, globe-trotting energy to the franchise. Mace's physicality — he performed many of his own stunts — and easy humour made him a popular Ward, particularly internationally. Dead Reckoning (1982) demonstrated surprising dramatic range, with Mace turning in a restrained, procedural performance at odds with his public reputation.

Tensions with director Victor Ashmore during Dead Reckoning were well documented, and Mace made it publicly known he had considered leaving the franchise. He remained for Shallow Graves (1986), which is generally considered his finest film, before departing. He has worked consistently since in television, and at 79 remains a popular guest at CIPHER fan conventions.

Personal life and controversies

Mace's personal life has been considerably more turbulent than his professional one. His first marriage to actress Linda Carrow ended acrimoniously in 1978 following tabloid reports of affairs on location during The Ivory Gambit. A second marriage, to Italian model Francesca Miretti, collapsed in 1990, partly attributed to Mace's heavy drinking during a fallow period in his career.

In 1993, a woman named Carole Dennett publicly claimed that Mace had fathered her son, born in 1982. Mace initially denied the claim. A 1994 DNA test confirmed paternity. He issued a public statement acknowledging the child and has maintained a relationship with him since. The episode was covered extensively by the British tabloid press and referenced obliquely in the Sixty Years in the Cold documentary.

Mace has spoken openly about his past struggles with alcohol, crediting a 1995 rehabilitation programme and his third wife, former civil servant Helen Bryce, with his recovery. He has been sober since 1996 and is an active supporter of addiction recovery charities.
